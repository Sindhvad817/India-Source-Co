## The traditional pieces

- **Diyas:** small oil lamps, traditionally clay, often hand-painted or decorated with mirrors and beads. Lighting them is at the heart of Diwali.
- **Tea-light holders:** a practical way to bring diya-style light to every surface. The holders pictured are wrapped in colored thread with a woven gold ribbon pattern.
- **Torans:** decorative hangings for doorways, made from fabric, beads, mirrors, or artificial marigolds and mango leaves, that welcome guests.
- **Rangoli:** colorful patterns at the entrance. Beyond traditional colored powder, there are stencils and reusable rangoli mats that last for years.
- **Lanterns (akash kandil):** hanging paper or fabric lanterns for windows, balconies, and porches.
- **Garlands:** fresh or artificial marigold strings for doorways, railings, and the puja area.
- **Urli bowls:** wide, shallow bowls filled with water, floating flowers, and candles, often used as a centerpiece.

## Planning by space

Working space by space makes it easier to know how many pieces you need:

- **Entrance:** a toran over the door, a rangoli or rangoli mat at the threshold, and diyas along the steps
- **Puja area:** diyas, a decorated thali, and fresh or artificial flowers
- **Living and dining areas:** tea-light holders, an urli centerpiece, and garlands
- **Windows and balconies:** lanterns and string lights that are visible from outside

Photos of your space help us suggest pieces that fit it.

## Real flame or LED?

Real diyas and candles are traditional, and many families keep them for the puja itself. Whatever you choose, never leave a flame unattended, keep it away from curtains, garlands, and other fabric, and place it out of reach of children and pets. LED diyas and tea lights are a sensible alternative for windows, shelves, apartments with rules about open flames, and homes with little ones.

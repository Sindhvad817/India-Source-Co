## What "custom" can mean for a lehenga

A lehenga has three parts — the flared skirt (the lehenga itself), the fitted blouse (choli), and the dupatta — and a custom request can touch any of them. Some people want a completely new design. Others love an existing style but need a different color, a modest neckline, longer sleeves, or a lighter skirt they can dance in. Telling us which parts matter most helps us focus on the right designers and artisans. What's possible for each change depends on who makes the piece, so we confirm the details with them before you commit.

## Choosing a fabric

Fabric decides how a lehenga moves, how warm it feels, and how formal it reads.

- **Silk and brocade** (including Banarasi-style weaves) carry woven motifs, hold their shape, and suit weddings and formal events. The lehenga pictured above uses woven gold motifs.
- **Georgette and chiffon** are light and fluid. They're comfortable for long evenings, warm venues, and a sangeet with plenty of dancing.
- **Velvet** feels rich and substantial, and is popular for winter celebrations.
- **Net and organza** create sheer, layered looks and are often paired with embroidery.
- **Raw silk** has a crisp texture and holds pleats well.

If you're unsure, describe the setting — indoor or outdoor, season, how long you'll wear it — and we'll suggest options from the fabrics our suppliers can offer for your request.

## Embroidery and embellishment

Indian lehengas are known for their handwork. Which techniques are available for your piece depends on the artisans we work with for your request, but common ones include **zari** (metallic thread), **zardozi** (raised metallic embroidery), **gota patti** (appliqué with metallic ribbon), **mirror work**, **resham** (silk thread embroidery), and **sequins**. Two practical points are worth keeping in mind:

- **Weight:** heavier embellishment means a heavier garment. If you'll wear the lehenga for many hours, say so.
- **Time:** dense handwork takes longer to complete than lighter detailing, so it affects how early you should start.

## Silhouette, blouse, and dupatta

A lehenga skirt can be **A-line**, fully **flared** (often called kalidar, made from many panels), **mermaid** (fitted through the hips), or straight and panelled. For the blouse, think about the neckline, back design, sleeve length, and how much coverage you want. The dupatta can be draped many ways; some brides wear two — one over the head and one across the shoulders. Saving photos that show the back and the drape, not just the front, makes your request much clearer.

## What to include in your request

The more of this you can share, the faster we can come back with useful options:

- **The occasion and date**, plus any other events you'll attend
- **Inspiration photos**, ideally showing the front, back, and close-ups of the embroidery
- **Colors**, with photos taken in daylight if you're matching something (exact color matches depend on available fabrics and are confirmed with the supplier)
- **Fabric and embellishment preferences** — and anything you definitely don't want
- **Sizing:** your usual size, or measurements if you have them
- **Quantity**, if you're ordering for bridesmaids or family
- **A budget range**

## Tips if you're taking your own measurements

Have someone help you, measure over the undergarments you plan to wear, and keep the tape snug but not tight. For skirt length, wear the heels you expect to wear on the day. We'll confirm exactly which measurements are needed for your chosen option.

## Planning around your date

We don't publish fixed timelines, because they depend on the fabric, the handwork, the number of outfits, and delivery. The best thing you can do is start early and include your date in your request. We'll tell you what's realistic before you approve anything.

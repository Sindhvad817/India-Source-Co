## Sherwani, bandhgala, or Indo-western?

Choosing the right style for each event matters more than any single detail.

- **Sherwani:** a long, coat-like garment, usually knee-length or longer, worn over a kurta with fitted churidar or straight trousers. It's the traditional choice for a groom on the wedding day and often carries the most embroidery.
- **Bandhgala or jodhpuri:** a shorter, structured jacket with a closed band collar. It reads as formal but more restrained, and works well for receptions, family members of the couple, and guests.
- **Indo-western:** cuts that borrow from both traditions, such as an asymmetric closure, a longline jacket, or an open layered look like the one pictured, which pairs an embroidered jacket with a plain kurta.
- **Kurta set with a jacket:** lighter and easier to move in, and popular for mehndi and sangeet events.

## Fabric and embroidery

**Silk, brocade, and jacquard** have woven patterns and a formal sheen. **Velvet** is rich and warm for winter weddings. **Raw silk** gives a crisp, structured look. **Linen and cotton blends** are lighter and suit daytime or warm-weather events.

Embroidery can be dense or minimal. **Zardozi** (raised metallic thread) is the most formal. **Resham** (silk thread) can create colorful floral work like the jacket pictured. **Tone-on-tone** thread embroidery adds texture without competing with the bride's outfit. Mention how much detail you're comfortable wearing; it changes the options we look for. Specific fabrics and embroidery techniques depend on the artisans and suppliers available for your order, and we'll confirm them in your quote. ## Details that change the look

- **Collar:** most sherwanis use a mandarin or band collar; the height and stiffness vary.
- **Buttons:** decorative buttons are an easy way to personalize, from gold metal to enamel or fabric-covered, depending on what the maker can source.
- **Bottoms:** churidar (fitted and gathered at the ankle), straight trousers, or dhoti-style pants.
- **Accessories:** a stole or dupatta, a safa (turban), mojari or jutti shoes, and a brooch. You can ask about accessories in the same request, and we'll confirm which ones can be sourced.

## Coordinating with the wedding

If you're the groom, send a photo of your partner's outfit, or at least its colors. Some couples match exactly; others choose a complementary shade or keep the sherwani neutral and let one accent color tie the two together. Families often coordinate across several events, so it helps to list every event you're dressing for.

## What to include in your request

- **The occasion(s) and date**
- **Inspiration photos**, including close-ups of collars, embroidery, and buttons you like
- **Colors**, and your partner's or family's palette if you're coordinating
- **Style preference**: sherwani, bandhgala, Indo-western, or not sure yet
- **Sizing:** your usual jacket size, or measurements if you have them
- **Quantity**, if you're ordering for groomsmen or family
- **A budget range**

## A note on fit

Structured garments depend most on the shoulders and chest, so measurements there matter. Think about comfort, too: many ceremonies involve sitting on low seating or the floor for long stretches, so mention it if you want a little more ease. We'll confirm the measurements we need for your chosen option before production begins.

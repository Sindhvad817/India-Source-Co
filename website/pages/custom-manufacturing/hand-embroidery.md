## Techniques and where they come from

- **Zardozi:** raised embroidery in metallic thread, often with sequins and beads, traditionally associated with Lucknow, Agra, and Delhi. It's heavy and formal, and widely used in bridal wear.
- **Aari:** a chain stitch worked with a hooked needle on a frame, used for fine continuous lines and dense fills.
- **Chikankari:** delicate white-on-white or pastel shadow work on fine cotton or georgette, from Lucknow.
- **Mirror work (shisha):** small mirrors held in place with decorative stitching, associated with Gujarat and Rajasthan.
- **Kantha:** layered running-stitch work associated with West Bengal.
- **Phulkari:** bold floral darning-stitch work in silk floss, from Punjab.
- **Gota patti:** appliqué with metallic ribbon, associated with Rajasthan.
- **Resham, beading, and sequins:** silk-thread embroidery and frame beadwork, often combined with the techniques above.

## What can be embroidered

- **Yardage or panels** that you cut and sew yourself
- **Placement embroidery** on garment pieces cut to your pattern before they're sewn
- **Finished garments**, embroidered and assembled
- **Components** such as yokes, borders, necklines, motifs, and appliqués
- **Home décor** such as cushion covers, table runners, and wall hangings

## How to specify a project

- **Artwork at actual size**, as vector files or clear drawings with dimensions
- **Placement**, marked on your pattern pieces or a photo of the garment
- **Technique**, or the effect you want if you're unsure
- **Materials:** silk, rayon, cotton, or metallic thread; beads, sequins, mirrors, or stones
- **Colors**, ideally as physical swatches, for each colorway
- **Base fabric:** type, weight, and color, and whether you'll supply it
- **Coverage:** how dense or open the design should be
- **Finishing:** backing or interfacing and how pieces should be packed
- **Quantities** per design and per colorway

## Hand or machine embroidery?

Hand embroidery is right when you want raised texture, mixed materials, fine detail on curved shapes, or the character of handwork. Machine embroidery is right for identical repeats, logos, small lettering, and large volumes at a consistent cost. Many products combine the two, with machine embroidery for outlines and hand embellishment on top.

## Sampling and natural variation

A sample, often called a strike-off, shows how a supplier interprets your artwork, colors, and density before production. Expect small differences between hand-embroidered pieces, and agree on the approved sample as the reference for the whole order. Samples are arranged where a supplier offers them, and their availability is confirmed in your quote.

## Planning time for handwork

Hand embroidery is labor-intensive, and dense or metallic work takes longer than open designs. Include your target date in your request so we can tell you whether it's realistic.
